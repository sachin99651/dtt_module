American Sign Language (ASL)

The primary sign language used by the Deaf community in the United States and parts of Canada. ASL has its own grammar that is distinct from English — sentences follow a topic-comment structure. It uses handshapes, movement, and facial expressions to convey meaning.

British Sign Language
BSL

Used in the UK, BSL is a completely different language from ASL despite both being used in English-speaking countries. It uses a two-handed fingerspelling alphabet.

Two-handed ~150k users UK

Indian Sign Language
ISL

Used across India with regional variations. ISL has similarities to BSL and is recognized as one of the official languages for the Deaf community in India.

French Sign Language
LSF

LSF is historically significant — ASL was heavily influenced by it when Thomas Gallaudet brought Laurent Clerc to America in the 1800s.

International Sign
IS

A pidgin sign language used at international events. It borrows from multiple sign languages and relies heavily on gesture and mime for cross-cultural communication.

Japanese Sign Language
JSL / NSL

Used by Japan's Deaf community, JSL has a unique grammar system and shares little overlap with spoken Japanese or with ASL/BSL families.

Unique grammar ~320k users Japan

Auslan
AUSLAN

Australian Sign Language shares roots with BSL and New Zealand Sign Language. It uses a two-handed fingerspelling system and is the primary language of the Australian Deaf community.

BSL-derived ~16k users Australia
